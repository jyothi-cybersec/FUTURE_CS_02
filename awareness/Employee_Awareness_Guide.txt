PHISHING EMAIL AWARENESS GUIDE

PURPOSE

Phishing emails attempt to trick users into clicking malicious links,
sharing credentials, opening unsafe attachments, or taking an action that
benefits an attacker.

HOW TO IDENTIFY A SUSPICIOUS EMAIL

1. Check the sender
Look at the complete sender address, not only the display name. Be
careful when the domain does not match the organization being claimed.

2. Check the message for urgency
Attackers often create pressure with account-lock warnings, security
alerts, deadlines, or threats.

3. Inspect links before clicking
Check where a link is intended to go. Be cautious with unfamiliar domains,
shortened URLs, and links that do not match the organization being
claimed.

4. Look for requests for sensitive information
Be suspicious of unexpected requests for passwords, OTPs, payment
information, account details, or other confidential information.

5. Check authentication indicators when available
SPF, DKIM, and DMARC results can provide useful evidence during technical
email investigation.

6. Verify independently
If an email claims there is an account problem, open the organization's
known official website manually or use a trusted support channel instead
of using the email link.

EMPLOYEE DO'S

- Verify the sender's complete email address.
- Check suspicious links before clicking.
- Treat unexpected urgent requests cautiously.
- Report suspected phishing emails through the organization's approved
  reporting process.
- Verify account or payment requests using a trusted channel.
- Contact IT/security when unsure.

EMPLOYEE DON'TS

- Do not click suspicious links.
- Do not enter passwords after following an unexpected email link.
- Do not share OTPs or authentication codes.
- Do not open unexpected attachments.
- Do not rely only on logos, branding, or display names.
- Do not allow urgency to replace verification.

IF YOU CLICKED A SUSPICIOUS LINK

1. Stop entering information.
2. Close the suspicious webpage.
3. Inform the organization's IT/security team immediately.
4. If credentials were entered, follow the organization's password-reset
   and incident-response procedure.
5. Report the original email so it can be investigated.

KEY RULE

When an email creates urgency and asks you to perform a security-sensitive
action, pause and verify the request independently before taking action.
